## How

The whole source is the organizing object. Chapters account for the recording, and deliverables attach through their source ranges. The shared-boundary control changes the end of one chapter and the start of its neighbour together.

Mode switching changes the available tools around the work. The floating glass dock expands its labels, while cut controls and evidence use stable surfaces. Formal approval stays separate from resolving a feedback note.

The prototype uses fictional project data and an original vector illustration for its media frame. In this first iteration, no customer footage, customer names, uploads, AI calls, or publishing were used. Its playhead is simulated and its processing/check results are sample states.

## Went wrong

- The first layout put the mode dock below the fold on a laptop-sized viewport. Made the dock persistent and tightened the compact layout; editing content can scroll with bottom clearance.
- Very short chapters made a time ruler imply more precision than the overview layout supported. Labeled the overview's expanded short ranges explicitly; exact ranges remain available in the list and source details.
- The first client preview retained the agency navigation around it. Removed that chrome from the client surface and checked the result at 360px width.
- Caught a stale kept-duration/count readout after restoring the proposed drop. Derived the coverage total and chapter count from the local state.

## LinkedIn draft

The first Temnia interface didn't feel right to me.

It explained the chapter structure, but I wanted to feel like I was entering an editing room. So I asked for a rethink.

The new concept puts a large source monitor in the centre. The chapter reel stays underneath. A floating panel becomes a transcript, a review surface, or a feedback conversation depending on the work mode. The tool dock is separate from those modes, so captions and sound are tools inside the room.

There's also a canvas for seeing how the source, chapters, and deliverables connect.

This is still an interactive concept. The interview frame is generated, playback is simulated, and no media pipeline runs behind it. I have local checks for the interactions, including blocking delivery after an approved edit changes. I still need to decide whether the experience feels right before taking it further.
