# Naming — replacing MITOSIA

**Status:** v1.0, 2026-09-03 — candidates generated after the class-42 conflict on `Mitosia` ([trademark-filing.md](trademark-filing.md) §8)
**Method:** ~230 candidates across cutting / selecting / dividing / provenance / craft roots in Greek, Latin, Japanese, Sanskrit and English; `.com` checked by DNS then Verisign whois (`No match for`); finalists web-searched for existing brands. Availability is a snapshot — a coined `.com` costs ~₹1,000, so register the top three the day a direction is chosen and drop the losers after the trademark search.

## What a good name has to do here

1. **Be coined.** The Mitosia lesson: the strongest marks are invented words. Real words and simple compounds are gone as `.com`s and crowded as marks.
2. **Carry the product's idea** — cutting with precision, choosing the best, one source becoming many, and everything provable — without *describing* software (a Section 9 objection).
3. **Say cleanly on first sight**, two or three syllables, no ambiguous vowels.
4. **Sit far from any class 9/42 mark phonetically.** That is the check that killed Mitosia, and it must run on the IP India Public Search (Phonetic) before anything is announced.

## Recommendation

Three names are both meaningful and clean on every check so far: **Ekloga**, **Decerpa**, **Lapidia**. **Temnia** is the best-sounding brand of the set and carries only a low-grade Temenos question. **Kaesura** has the best story — it names the exact editorial problem — but Kaseya is a real phonetic neighbour in the same class and must be searched before it is loved.

Suggested order: register `temnia.com`, `ekloga.com`, `kaesura.com` today (~₹3,000 total); run all five through the IP India Phonetic search in classes 9 and 42 plus WIPO Global Brand Database; choose from whatever survives; let the other two domains lapse.

## Greek roots, deeper (2026-09-03)

Mitosia came from *mitos* (μίτος, thread — Ariadne's clew, the warp of the loom, the thread-like chromosomes of mitosis). The replacement should come from the same tradition and say something true about the product. 145 Greek-rooted forms checked; the single-word forms (dokimos, tekmor, kairos, epitomē, syntomos, akribeia) are gone, but the morphological siblings are largely free.

### A. The cut — τέμνω, Temnia's own family

Temnia (*temnō*, "I cut") sits in a family that gave English *atom* (ἄτομος, that which cannot be cut further), *epitome* (ἐπιτομή, cut short → the essence), *anatomy*, *dichotomy*, and Greek *syntomos* (σύντομος, "cut together" → concise). Temnia names the act; its siblings name the quality of the act.

## Brand story — what Temnia means

**The word.** Temnia is coined, not attested. It is built from the Greek verb *temnō* (τέμνω, "I cut") with the Greek abstract-noun ending *-ia* (*sophia*, *philia*, *akribeia*), so it reads as "the cutting" / "the art of the cut." The attested nouns from the root are *tomē* (a cut), *tmēma* (a segment), *tmēsis* (cutting). **Say "from the Greek *temnō*," never "means X in Greek."** Coined is a feature: coined marks get the widest protection.

**1. Every child of the root names a layer of the product.**

| Word | Greek | Literal | In Temnia |
|---|---|---|---|
| atom | ἄτομος | un-cuttable | the sentence — the model picks sentence IDs, never cuts inside one |
| epitome | ἐπιτομή | cut short → the essence | the clip |
| anatomy | ἀνατομή | cutting up, to understand | the transcript substrate |
| tome | τόμος | a cut; a section of a scroll | the chapter |
| dichotomy | διχοτομία | cut in two | keep / drop |

**2. Cutting as setting apart.** *Temenos* (τέμενος), the sacred precinct, is literally "a piece cut off" — land carved out of the ordinary for a god or a king; in Jung, the protected space where transformation happens. In Greek the deepest sense of cutting is consecration, not loss. That is the moments lane.

**3. The thread and the shears.** Mitosia was *mitos*, the thread. Temnia is the cut. Clotho spins, Lachesis measures, Atropos — "the inflexible" — cuts, and her cut cannot be undone. Which is why the software never makes that cut alone: a human decides every cut that ships. *The thread was the first name. The cut is the second. The hand on the shears is still yours.*

**4. To cut a path.** *Temnein hodon* — "to cut a road," to make a way where there was none. A two-hour recording is a wilderness.

**5. Plato's carver.** *Phaedrus* 265e: divide "at the natural joints, not hacking like a bad carver." The standard, stated 2,400 years ago; what the 18% got wrong.

**6. Cross-language check (2026-09-03).** The word carries two meanings in the world, not one. **Greek:** *temnō*, to cut / to intersect (the intended one). **Slavic:** *temno* = "dark" — Russian and Ukrainian *темно*, Czech/Slovak *temno*, Slovene *temno*, Serbo-Croatian *tamno*; Czech *Temno* is Jirásek's 1915 novel of Bohemia's "Age of Darkness"; Russian *темница* / Czech *temnice* = dungeon; Czech *temná komora* / Polish *ciemnia* = **the photographic darkroom** — where raw film becomes the finished image, which is the better neighbour to lean on ("In Greek, the cut. In Czech, the darkroom."). Rule: **never shorten the brand to "Temno"** in a Slavic market or on merch — Temnia is not a Slavic word, Temno is. Elsewhere clean: Japanese (テムニア, no meaning; faint sound-neighbour *tennō*, the Emperor), Latin (*temnō* = I despise, root of *contempt* — pedants only), Romance (*temo* = I fear, only if shortened), Sanskrit/Hindi (no meaning; *tamas* "darkness" shares the ancient Indo-European dark-root with the Slavic word, a different root from Greek *temnō*; Urdu/Hindi *tamannā*, a wish, is a mild positive neighbour), Arabic, Turkish, Chinese, Korean, German, French — nothing.
