# Filing the TEMNIA word mark in India

> **2026-09-03:** MITOSIA was abandoned after the class-42 conflict in §8. The mark is now **TEMNIA** (final 2026-09-03 — see [naming.md](naming.md)); §8 is kept as the record of why. Everything else in this guide applies unchanged.

**Status:** v1.0, 2026-09-03 — procedural guide for item 3 of [build-in-public.md](build-in-public.md) §11
**Not legal advice.** This is the process as published by IP India, written down so the filing can be done without re-researching it each time. Where the guide says *get an opinion*, get one.

## 0. Why a word mark, and why now

A **word mark** protects the word MITOSIA itself in any font, colour, or styling. A device (logo) mark protects only that artwork. For a name you intend to defend, the word mark is the one that matters — file it first; a logo mark can follow later if the logo becomes distinctive on its own.

The reason to file before Day 0 is the **priority date**. Registration takes 12–24 months, but the date that decides who wins a conflict is the *filing* date, not the registration date. Filing today means anyone who adopts MITOSIA after today is the infringer, even if their product launches first. That is the entire value of doing this before the series starts generating search traffic.

TEMNIA is a coined word (from Greek *temnō*, "I cut" — the root of *atom* and *epitome*). Coined words sit in the strongest distinctiveness category, so a **Section 9** objection (descriptive / non-distinctive) is unlikely. The real risk is **Section 11** — an earlier similar mark — which is what the search in step 2 is for.

## 2. Search before you file — do this first

Two questions to answer: (a) is there an earlier Indian mark that blocks MITOSIA, and (b) is there a foreign MITOSIA that will block the US/EU filings later. Both matter *before* you commit the name publicly.

### India — IP India public search

IP India runs **two different tools** that are easy to confuse. For a conflict search you want the first one:

| Tool | URL | What it is |
|---|---|---|
| **Public Search** ← *use this* | `tmrsearch.ipindia.gov.in/tmrpublicsearch/frmmain.aspx` | Search all marks **by word**. No login. CAPTCHA on each search. |
| E-Register & Application Status | `tmrsearch.ipindia.gov.in/eregister/` | Look up **one known application number** for its full status and prosecution history. Needs a login. |

The E-Register is the wrong tool for step 2 — it answers "what is the status of application 1234567", not "does anything like MITOSIA already exist". It becomes the right tool in step 5, for tracking our own applications once filed.

On the Public Search page, the search type is chosen from a dropdown — **Wordmark** (with *Start With* / *Contains* / *Match With*) and **Phonetic** are separate options; run both. Each search needs the CAPTCHA re-entered, so work through the list methodically and screenshot anything relevant.

Run each of these and record what you find:

| Search type | Term | Class |
|---|---|---|
| Wordmark → **Phonetic** | `MITOSIA` | 9, then 42, then leave class blank for all classes |
| Wordmark → **Start With** | `MITOS` | 9, 42 |
| Wordmark → **Contains** | `MITOSI` | blank (all classes) |
| Wordmark → **Phonetic** | `MITOSIS` | 9, 42 |
| Wordmark → **Phonetic** | `MYTOSIA`, `MITOZIA`, `MITOSSIA` | blank |

Also check classes **35** (advertising, marketing, business services) and **41** (entertainment, production of audio/video content) — an agency-adjacent product can collide with marks there even though we are not filing in them.

**Reading the results.** For each hit, note the mark, class, status, and proprietor. Status matters:

- *Registered* or *Objected/Opposed* in class 9 or 42, phonetically close → real risk. Get an attorney opinion before filing.
- *Abandoned*, *Withdrawn*, *Refused* → generally not a bar, though a registered mark that lapsed recently can still be revived.
- Close mark in an unrelated class (pharma, biotech — likely, given *mitosis*) → usually fine. Trademark rights are class-specific.

Expect hits on MITOSIS-derived marks in classes 1, 5, 10 and 44 (biotech, medical). Those do not block a software mark.

### Elsewhere — before the name is public

- **USPTO** — `tmsearch.uspto.gov`, search MITOSIA and MITOSIS in classes 9 and 42.
- **EUIPO** — `euipo.europa.eu` eSearch, same terms.
- **WIPO Global Brand Database** — `branddb.wipo.int` covers many registries at once; the fastest single check.
- A plain web search for "Mitosia" — an unregistered but actively-used mark can still generate a common-law claim in some jurisdictions.

If all four come back clean, the name is safe to commit to publicly.

## 3. Draft the specification (goods and services)

Filed per class. Too narrow and a competitor works around it; too broad and the examiner objects. This wording is drafted for what the PRD describes — review it against the PRD before filing.

**Class 9** (goods — software as a product):

> Downloadable and recorded computer software for artificial intelligence-based analysis, transcription, curation, editing and rendering of audio and video content; downloadable computer software for media asset management, workflow automation, and review and approval of digital media assets; downloadable computer software for automated generation of video clips, captions, subtitles and written content from recorded audio-visual material.

**Class 42** (services — SaaS, the primary class for Mitosia):

> Software as a service (SaaS) featuring software for artificial intelligence-based analysis, transcription, curation, editing, rendering, scheduling and publishing of audio and video content; platform as a service (PaaS) featuring computer software platforms for content operations, media asset management and review and approval workflows; providing temporary use of non-downloadable software for generating video clips, captions, subtitles and written content from recorded audio-visual material; design, development and maintenance of computer software; hosting of digital media content.

**Use basis:** the product is unlaunched, so file as **"Proposed to be used"**. That needs no evidence and no affidavit. (Claiming a prior use date would require a user affidavit and supporting proof — not applicable, and don't claim it.)

## 4. File Form TM-A

Portal: **ipindiaonline.gov.in/trademarkefiling** — "Comprehensive e-Filing Services for Trade Marks".

What you need before you start:

1. **Class III Digital Signature Certificate** in the applicant's name. Required to sign the e-filing. Issued by licensed CAs (eMudhra, Sify, Capricorn and others), roughly ₹1,500–3,000 for 1–2 years, a day or two to issue with video KYC. *This is the one step that has lead time — start it first.*
2. **User registration** on the e-filing portal, with the DSC attached to the account.
3. Applicant details — name, full address, nationality. For an individual: name as on PAN/Aadhaar.
4. The mark: **TEMNIA**, entered as text. A word mark needs no image upload.
5. The two specifications from step 3.
6. Payment — net banking/card, ₹9,000 for two classes as an individual.

Filing options: one application covering both classes (a *multi-class* application, one number, fee still per class), or two single-class applications. **Two separate single-class applications is the safer choice** — if class 9 draws an objection, class 42 (the one that actually matters for a SaaS product) proceeds independently instead of being held up behind it.

You receive an **application number and filing receipt immediately**. From that moment you may use **™** next to the name. **®** is only lawful after the registration certificate issues — using it before is an offence, so don't.

If you use an attorney or registered TM agent, they file under a **Form TM-48** power of attorney and use their own DSC — you do not need one.

## 5. What happens next, and the deadlines that bite

| Stage | Typical timing | Your deadline |
|---|---|---|
| Filing receipt, application number | Immediate | — |
| Formality check | Weeks | Fix defects when notified |
| **Examination report** issued | 3–12 months | **Reply within 30 days of receipt.** Missing this can get the application treated as abandoned. |
| Show-cause hearing (if the reply doesn't clear it) | Varies | Attend or be represented |
| Publication in the Trade Marks Journal | After acceptance | — |
| **Opposition window** | **4 months from publication** | Anyone may oppose; you file a counter-statement within 2 months of receiving a notice |
| Registration certificate | 12–24 months total, if unopposed | Valid 10 years, renewable |

Two more dates worth putting in a calendar the day you file:

- **6 months from the Indian filing date** — the Paris Convention priority window. File in the US, EU, or via the Madrid Protocol within it and those applications inherit the Indian filing date. Miss it and you file abroad at whatever date you get. Relevant given the PRD's US ambitions; decide at M2 whether US revenue is close enough to justify the cost.
- **Journal publication date + 4 months** — when the mark is finally safe from opposition.

An examination report is normal, not a failure. Most first reports raise Section 9 or Section 11 points; a written reply distinguishing the cited marks resolves many of them.

## 6. DIY or attorney

**DIY is genuinely feasible** for a coined word mark with a clean search. The filing itself is a form.

**Use an attorney if** the step-2 search turns up a close mark in class 9/42, or you get an examination report citing earlier marks. Replying to an examination report is advocacy, not form-filling, and it is where the application is won or lost. Professional fees for a two-class filing typically run a few thousand to ~₹15,000 per class depending on the firm; an examination reply is usually billed separately.

A middle path that works well: **file yourself** (₹9,000 + DSC), and budget for an attorney only if an objection arrives.

|---|---|
| Application no. | **7804137** |
| Wordmark | **Mitosia** — identical to ours |
| Class | **42** — our primary class |
| Filed | **20/06/2026** — earlier than any application we can now make |
| Used since | **10/10/2024** — claims ~2 years of prior use |
| Proprietor | Saakshi Pilliwar, trading as MITOSIA |
| Status | Ready for Examination (live, progressing) |
| Form | Composite/device — a star logo with the word `mitosia` |
| Specification | Designing; graphic designing; design services for logo, brand, packaging, animation, map, clothing, fashion, brochure, jewellery; **designing of webpages, electronic systems, computer program**; packaging and wrapping materials; design consultancy |

**Why this is serious.** Identical word, identical class, earlier filing date, and a prior-use claim running back to October 2024. On every axis that decides a conflict, we are the **junior party** — including the domain: `mitosia.com` was registered 2026-07-30, after both their filing and their claimed first use. If we file in class 42 the examiner will almost certainly cite 7804137 under Section 11; even if we cleared examination they can oppose within the four-month window; and Section 34 gives a genuine prior user rights we cannot match with a "proposed to be used" application.

**What cuts the other way** — enough to be worth one professional opinion, not enough to proceed on:

- It appears to be a **composite/device mark**, which protects a narrower thing than a pure word mark would.
- Their actual services are **design services**, not AI content software. A services-dissimilarity argument exists — though it is weakened by "designing of webpages, electronic systems, computer program" in their specification, and by the fact that our target customers *are* agencies, which puts us in the same commercial world rather than a distant one.
- No web presence was found for a Mitosia design studio on 2026-09-03 (weak evidence; small studios are often Instagram- or Behance-only).
- It has not been examined yet and could still be objected to or refused on its own merits.

**Options**

1. **Change the name.** Cheapest now — the cost is a domain and zero audience. It becomes very expensive after the build-in-public series has run for months, and ruinous after customers exist.
2. **Acquire or coexist.** Approach the proprietor **through an agent, never directly**, to buy the application by assignment or agree a coexistence split (they keep design services, we take software/SaaS on a narrowed specification). We already hold `mitosia.com`, so a clean acquisition would resolve everything at once. A direct approach from an obviously motivated buyer raises the price.
3. **File anyway and fight.** Weakest position. The failure mode is rebranding at M2 with design partners on board — the scenario option 1 exists to avoid.
