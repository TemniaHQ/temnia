# Temnia: founder and product account strategy

**Version:** 2.0 · **Reviewed:** 2026-09-08

This revises the supplied two-account strategy. Account separation is a sensible editorial choice: people can follow Rajesh's decisions or Temnia's editing workflow for different reasons. It is not evidence that two accounts will grow faster than one. The [daily playbook](TEMNIA_BUILD_IN_PUBLIC_GUIDE.md) owns cadence, evidence and review; the [research review](RESEARCH_REVIEW_2026-09-08.md) explains the assessment.

## 1. Give each account a reason to exist

| | Founder X — @RajeshBuilds | Product X — @TemniaHQ |
|---|---|---|
| Reader promise | Useful lessons from the person building Temnia. | Understand the long-recording workflow Temnia is being built to improve. |
| Primary readers | Developers, founders and interested editors/creators. | Editors, podcast/video producers, agencies and long-form creators. |
| Core material | Decisions, experiments, mistakes, tradeoffs and the human/agent division of work. | Chapter/segment workflow, honest previews, useful suggestions, research questions and access status. |
| Voice | First person, specific, conversational. | Plain and approachable; “Temnia” for the product and “you” for the reader. |
| Proof | Actual decisions, scoped tests, code or permitted observations. | A visible interaction or output, a clearly labelled intention, or a genuine question. |
| Success | Relevant discussion and trust in Rajesh's judgment. | Product understanding, editing-workflow learning and qualified interest. |
| Weak default | Every post advertises the product or lists tools. | Every post repeats a founder announcement or sounds like a corporate changelog. |

Keep the broader RajeshBuilds identity. Other real work can belong there; do not turn the personal profile into a second product logo. Use “I” for Rajesh rather than inventing a team. The product account can say that Rajesh is speaking when a personal reply needs that context.

LinkedIn remains on Rajesh's personal profile for this experiment. Its posts can explain editing-workflow implications or deeper engineering decisions; there is no requirement to force an agency sales angle into every topic.

## 3. One combined workload

Start with **one founder X daily, three product X weekly and five personal LinkedIn weekly**. Product days initially fall on Monday, Wednesday and Friday. On those days the baseline has two original X posts across the two identities; other days have one. The [playbook's trial](TEMNIA_BUILD_IN_PUBLIC_GUIDE.md#3-cadence-a-practical-trial) defines time limits and review points.

A second founder post is optional when it adds another useful takeaway. A code refactor with no demonstrated user consequence can support a founder post while the product account uses a genuine workflow question or an unused prior lesson.

The attachment's three-X daily allocation, two founder plus one product, is an expansion mode. It becomes useful when there is enough independent material and capacity to review/respond. The combined count must remain explicit; “three X posts” must not silently become three posts on each account.

Use simple labels in the shared daily log:

- `XP1`: founder's primary X story.
- `XP2`: optional distinct founder X story.
- `XH1`: the product-account story when due or independently warranted.
- `LI1`: the personal LinkedIn explanation.

Alternative hooks are variants, not extra posts. Ordinary replies and reposts are community work. A substantive quote post can fill an original-content slot if counted once and labelled accordingly. No automatic reciprocal share is required.

## 5. Six useful product formats

### Show one real interaction

Lead with the editing task, show the actual interaction, and name the relevant development/access boundary. An upload or transcript navigation preview is a foundation for the chapter workflow; it does not demonstrate automatic chapter creation or export.

```text
Development preview: [actual verified interaction].

[What the editor can inspect or do in this recording].

[Material availability or scope boundary].
```

The asset must exist before the post says “watch below.” Do not attach a polished mockup to a present-tense capability claim.

### Ask a specific workflow question

```text
When you chapter a long interview, do you mark topic changes as you watch, or make a first pass and decide on the cuts afterward?
```

Ask because the answer could change a decision or test. Record the response as one observation, not market validation. Avoid questions that embed invented editing-time statistics or imply everyone works the same way.

### Explain a product principle

```text
The goal for Temnia's chapter workflow: every part of the recording belongs to a chapter or an explicit decision to leave it out.

That makes a missing passage something to review, rather than something to overlook.
```

“The goal” matters here. A design rule is not proof that the shipped system enforces it.

### Teach a useful editing practice

```text
A chapter-review habit to try: play the end of one section and the start of the next together.

A cut can sound clean on one side while leaving the next section without its context.
```

This is a suggestion the reader can evaluate without Temnia. Do not claim it came from Temnia customers or quantify a time saving without evidence.

### Show feedback changing a decision

Use a real permitted observation, the actual decision it changed, and the visible result or next test. Do not write “agencies asked us” for one comment. Permission to discuss a workflow does not automatically authorize a name, logo, private message or client-video screenshot.

### Publish a progress or access note

Summarize one verified improvement, one material limitation and the appropriate next step. Product availability announcements belong here. A functioning access route, eligibility and actual capacity precede a beta invitation; the post never invents them.

## 6. Route from the evidence

| Underlying event | Product-account story | Founder-account story |
|---|---|---|
| Transcript navigation works on a tested build. | What the editor can inspect in the preview. | A distinct decision about navigation, revision or timing. |
| Boundary selection remains a design proposal. | Ask how editors judge the start/end together. | Explain the joint-boundary design and what would test it. |
| A retry preserves a draft in a tested case. | Show recovery if it is useful and visible. | Explain the race and the regression that caught it. |
| An internal refactor has no visible consequence. | Usually use a different story. | Explain the actual engineering tradeoff. |
| A measured operation improves. | A relevant, scoped task result with conditions. | The bottleneck, comparison and remaining cost. |
| Permitted feedback changes the workflow. | The observation and change in the editing task. | An independently supported lesson about the original assumption. |
| A real access milestone occurs. | Canonical availability, eligibility and route. | A personal reflection with additional substance. |

**A pair based on the same work can be distinct.** If a future test verifies a chapter boundary preview, the product post could demonstrate how to review both sides. The founder post could explain a particular incorrect assumption and the test that disproved it. Two copies of “we added chapter preview,” even in different tones, are still one announcement.

One event does not have to supply both accounts. The founder can discuss the recorded uploader research correction while the product account asks a chapter-workflow question. Start from the facts and the reader's need, rather than asking an agent to reword a finished founder post for the brand.

## 7. The first two product-account weeks

This is the first cycle under this strategy, not a reset of the founder journey or a statement that any post has gone out.

| Slot | Product story | Evidence needed |
|---|---|---|
| Week 1 Monday | Introduction and pin. | Reviewed positioning and correct identity. |
| Week 1 Wednesday | Question about the chaptering process. | A genuinely unresolved workflow question. |
| Week 1 Friday | One existing development preview. | Public-safe asset and checked environment; otherwise use a useful editing suggestion. |
| Week 2 Monday | Shared-boundary review suggestion. | Clear framing as a suggestion or supported observation. |
| Week 2 Wednesday | A limitation or product principle. | Current scope/decision evidence. |
| Week 2 Friday | What this cycle taught us, or a new preview. | Actual responses or verification; no invented “users told us.” |

The founder account carries its own lessons in parallel. Introduce the product account once; do not repeatedly ask the same followers to follow it. A quiet product day can be skipped or moved without a catch-up burst.

## 8. Connections and platform policy

Make ownership clear in the profiles and relevant conversations. Choose the account best placed to answer a real question. A selective founder quote of a product announcement can be useful when it adds a real explanation; it is not a compulsory launch ritual.

X permits separate accounts for distinct purposes, including personal and business uses, while restricting substantially similar cross-posting and artificial amplification. A transparent relationship is not a licence to manufacture engagement. [X authenticity policy](https://help.x.com/en/rules-and-policies/authenticity)

Do not set up reciprocal likes, canned replies, duplicate pitches or fake customer excitement between the accounts. Each post should have reader value even without a boost from the other identity. Link to an actual published target when quoting; do not invent a dependency URL.

Keep drafting separate from automated interaction. X's automation rules prohibit automated likes and non-API website scripting, restrict automated replies, and require prior explicit written approval from X for AI reply bots. Any later publishing integration needs its own current-policy review and authorized scope. This guide installs no integration. [X automation rules](https://help.x.com/en/rules-and-policies/x-automation)

Relevant search themes include chapter boundaries, long-form editing, podcast chapters, preserving context, transcript corrections and client revisions. Participate manually where there is something useful to contribute. Do not mass-follow, scrape follower lists, send unsolicited automated pitches or pressure a person into becoming a lead.

## 9. Evaluate each account's job

Use the [shared measurement process](TEMNIA_BUILD_IN_PUBLIC_GUIDE.md#12-measure-and-adjust). Keep one facts/drafts history and separate publication/account fields, not separate evidence databases.

For the founder account, inspect meaningful discussion and useful connections. For the product account, inspect whether relevant editors and creators understand the workflow, describe a concrete need, or take an actual access step when available. A small useful conversation can matter more than a large generic reaction count.

Do not compare raw impressions between a new product account and an established founder account as a quality contest. Record account age/size and subscription or distribution changes when known. Do not assume missing analytics means zero.

When a real website and analytics setup exist, use distinct labels for founder and product links, for example `utm_source=x`, `utm_medium=organic_social`, and an account-specific `utm_content`. These are proposed tracking labels, not existing instrumentation. They describe a clicked link, not every prior exposure or a causal conversion.

If the product account repeatedly borrows founder announcements, reduce its frequency and collect more workflow questions. If it attracts only builders, change the task language and conversations before increasing output. If the second account consumes time without a distinct purpose after a fair trial, maintain a useful profile/pin and publish only substantive updates until there is more to say.
