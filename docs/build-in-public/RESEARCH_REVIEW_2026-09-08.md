# Review of the Temnia build-in-public guides

**Reviewed:** 2026-09-08. Inputs: `TEMNIA_BUILD_IN_PUBLIC_GUIDE_v1_1.md` and `TEMNIA_TWO_ACCOUNT_STRATEGY.md`, supplied by Rajesh; repository strategy/decisions; the sources below.

**Verdict:** the guides provide a good editorial foundation. Keep evidence-based drafting, distinct reader takeaways, separate founder/product roles, truthful previews and qualified conversations. Revise the positioning, simplify the workflow and treat cadence as an experiment. The research does not establish that 2–4 X posts and 1–2 LinkedIn posts every day is the best strategy for Temnia.

Rajesh clarified that earlier posting habits were not based on proper research and asked to change them where warranted. Accordingly, the old mandatory opener, single-post cap and prohibition on customer framing are reassessed here rather than preserved as constraints. The revised recommendations are in the [playbook](TEMNIA_BUILD_IN_PUBLIC_GUIDE.md) and [account strategy](TEMNIA_TWO_ACCOUNT_STRATEGY.md).

## What the research can support

There are three different kinds of evidence:

1. **Platform rules and limits:** authoritative for permitted behaviour and composer constraints, but they do not reveal an exact ranking formula.
2. **Observational posting data:** useful for choosing experiments, with sampling, measurement and confounding limits.
3. **Temnia-specific editorial judgment:** account roles, workload, hooks and format choices. These need testing with Rajesh's audience.

No live social profiles, post analytics, conversion data or actual customer interviews were audited for this review. This assesses the guidance and designs a practical trial; it does not establish what has already worked for Rajesh.

### Frequency evidence needs a narrower interpretation

[Buffer's LinkedIn frequency article](https://buffer.com/resources/how-often-to-post-on-linkedin/) reports analysis of over two million posts from more than 94,000 accounts, using within-account comparisons and fixed effects. It recommends 2–5 weekly posts as a sustainable starting range and associates higher frequency with stronger results. The page displays August 28, 2025 despite its 2026 title. It is not a randomized test, a founder-specific sample or evidence of Temnia customer acquisition.

The linked [original analyst's write-up](https://symphonious-shortbread-cd2770.netlify.app/posts/linkedin-posting-frequency/), dated August 1, 2025, is more qualified: its narrative describes declining per-post reach at higher frequency despite engagement/total-reach gains, unlike the marketing summary's stronger reach claims. Its displayed SQL filters posts to `engagements > 5` and qualifying profiles to at least four active weeks. That excludes low-response observations; the frequency buckets therefore describe filtered posts in the shown query, not necessarily all output. The displayed outputs also warrant care about units. We did not rerun its data. Treat the inconsistency as a reason to avoid precise uplift forecasts or the assertion that more posting always improves each post.

[Buffer's 2026 engagement report](https://buffer.com/resources/state-of-social-media-engagement-2026/) explicitly discusses resource, momentum and platform-fit confounding. Its data associates consistent activity and replying with stronger outcomes, while acknowledging that these relationships cannot fully establish causation. It also reports different patterns for total growth and per-post reach. This supports a sustainable experiment and attention to conversations, not a guaranteed reply multiplier, ideal daily quota or advice to buy a subscription.

There is insufficient evidence here to transfer LinkedIn frequency estimates to X, prescribe separate quotas for two X accounts, or claim that a two-account setup improves conversion. Those are editorial/workload decisions.

### Substance has stronger support than formulaic volume

[LinkedIn's “Keeping conversations real”](https://news.linkedin.com/2026/keeping-conversations-real-on-linkedin) allows AI writing assistance while emphasizing real voice, perspective and substance. It describes reduced distribution of generic material and action against automated low-value comments. The pressroom version is dated June 4, 2026 and identifies an original May 20 article. This supports using agents to organize real evidence and then having Rajesh make the post his own; it does not support synthetic founder experiences or a claim that particular punctuation causes a penalty.

[X's organic best practices](https://business.x.com/en/basics/organic-best-practices) recommends concise, conversational copy and useful media practices. Its search-index text was accessible; direct retrieval returned 404 during this review. We use it only as qualitative guidance, not as the sole authority for a critical rule or evidence that a particular asset always wins.

## The adopted trial and why

Start with **one founder X daily, three product X weekly and five personal LinkedIn weekly**. Permit an additional founder post when it has a different supported takeaway. The baseline totals **10 X + 5 LinkedIn per week**.

This maintains the founder's existing ability to document daily work, gives the product account enough distinct material to evaluate its purpose, and keeps LinkedIn at the upper end of the source's moderate starting range. It is our workload judgment; the evidence does not prove these precise numbers are optimal.

Begin with a **30-minute daily editorial/community budget**, counting additional capture time in the weekly total. Review feasibility at **14 days**, then audience response at **28 days**, extending the trial if evidence is sparse. These intervals are review checkpoints, not promises of statistical confidence.

The supplied 2 personal + 1 product X daily model remains an expansion option when independent stories and time support it. Conversely, reduce output when evidence is thin or posting displaces building. Assess account-specific useful conversations, relevant access actions when available and time spent, alongside ordinary distribution metrics.

The trial has no start date or results until Rajesh actually begins publishing under it. Saving this review does not schedule posts, subscribe to a service, authenticate accounts or claim any growth.

## Platform reference checks

The references below were checked for their stated limited purpose, not treated as evidence of growth outcomes. Recheck rules before a later integration and composer limits when drafting actual copy.

| Official reference | Relevant supported boundary |
|---|---|
| [X: counting characters](https://docs.x.com/fundamentals/counting-characters) | Standard posts use a 280-character weighted limit; detected URLs count as 23, and Unicode/emoji handling matters. Use a compatible counter or composer. |
| [X: customize your profile](https://help.x.com/en/managing-your-account/how-to-customize-your-profile) | Profile bio limit of 160 characters, website field and pinning capability. |
| [X: authenticity](https://help.x.com/en/rules-and-policies/authenticity) | Distinct personal/business accounts are permitted; substantially similar cross-posting and artificial amplification are restricted. |
| [X: automation rules](https://help.x.com/en/rules-and-policies/x-automation) | Automated likes and non-API website scripting are prohibited; automated replies have conditions, including prior explicit written X approval for AI reply bots. |
| [LinkedIn: post and share updates](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a528176) | Standard feed posts have a 3,000-character limit. This does not establish a preferred word count. |
| [LinkedIn: content created with AI](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a1481496) | Review and take responsibility for assisted content. LinkedIn recommends disclosure when reliance on AI is substantial and not apparent; this is not a blanket disclosure requirement for minor editing. |

The reviewed guides' other algorithm/format sources are not carried forward as fresh verified support. There is no need to retain every citation when the associated claim is removed. No dependency or publishing integration is required to follow this guidance.
